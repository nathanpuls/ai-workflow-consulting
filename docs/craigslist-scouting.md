# Craigslist Scouting

Use this workflow to find Craigslist posts where Nathan Puls can offer project-based workflow cleanup help. The goal is not to apply for jobs as an employee. The goal is to find businesses already describing repetitive admin, operations, customer service, scheduling, tracking, or handoff problems, then send a specific, low-hype reply.

## Positioning

Nathan helps small businesses clean up repetitive admin work with simple systems that are easy to use.

Good language:

- "simple workflow"
- "one process"
- "repetitive admin work"
- "easy to lose track of"
- "simple system your team can actually use"
- "I am not applying as a full-time employee, but..."

Avoid leading with:

- "AI automation agency"
- "AI-powered transformation"
- "I can automate your whole business"
- generic automation pitches that do not mention the actual post

## Fit Criteria

A post is a good fit if it describes a repeatable process, information flow, tracking burden, customer/admin handoff, or coordination problem that could be partly systematized.

Strong signals:

- intake, registration, forms, applications, or onboarding
- scheduling, appointments, dispatch, shift coverage, or bookings
- quotes, estimates, invoices, payments, or order processing
- customer follow-up, lead follow-up, reviews, calls, emails, chat, or texts
- data entry, records, reports, spreadsheets, CRM, ERP, or Microsoft Office
- document organization, filing, receipts, certificates, permits, compliance
- inventory, product listings, shipping, logistics, warehouse coordination
- payroll inputs, timecards, attendance, check-ins, or recurring updates
- team handoffs where details may fall through the cracks

Weak or skip signals:

- big-company "Apply Now" funnels with no small-business operator visible
- resume-only employee applications with no room for project help
- posts that are mostly physical labor, cleaning, errands, or personal care
- posts where the pay is extremely low and there is no obvious process pain
- suspicious package receiving, reshipping, check handling, or too-good-to-be-true posts
- posts whose main purpose is adult/talent/modeling work
- other service providers advertising competing services

Low hourly pay can still be a signal, but handle it carefully. It may mean they are trying to solve an operations problem with cheap labor. Do not sound like Nathan is competing with a low hourly worker. Frame the reply as project-based help that may reduce repeated manual work.

## Second-Pass Review

Before drafting or sending anything, inspect the actual post closely. Do not rely only on search snippets.

For each candidate, capture:

- post title
- company or contact name, if shown
- post URL
- exact tasks/tools mentioned
- application method: Craigslist relay, direct email, phone/text, external form, or resume-only
- whether Nathan should send, maybe send, or skip
- why it fits
- any warning or awkwardness

Be especially careful about:

- posts that ask for a resume and brief introduction
- posts that require a Google Form or external job application
- posts that list low hourly pay
- posts that are clearly seeking a full-time employee
- posts where the correct contact email is hidden behind the Craigslist reply button

## Reply Address Process

Do not guess the email address.

For Craigslist relay posts:

1. Open the listing in Chrome or a browser where Craigslist works.
2. Click `reply`.
3. Expand `email`.
4. Copy the displayed address, usually ending in `@job.craigslist.org`.
5. Use the post title as the subject unless a better subject is obvious.

For direct-email posts:

1. Use the direct email shown in the listing or reply panel.
2. If the email appears only in an image, read it carefully and verify spelling.
3. If there are multiple contacts, use the one named in the post or the first relevant hiring/contact email.

For Google Forms or external forms:

1. Open the form before recommending it.
2. Inspect the required fields.
3. If it is clearly a job application with resume upload and hourly applicant questions, usually skip it unless the user explicitly wants to apply.
4. If answering, make clear that Nathan is offering project-based workflow setup, not applying as a regular hourly employee.

## Drafting Rules

Every reply must mention exact details from the post. Pull the business's own words into the message.

Good hooks:

- "The parts about quotes, invoices, order processing, data entry, warehouse coordination, and following up on estimates caught my eye."
- "The parts about employee check-ins, attendance, shift coverage, onboarding, payroll, records, and client updates caught my eye."
- "The parts about participant registration, scheduling, payments, certifications, and mailing certificates caught my eye."

Do not send a generic version if the post names specific tools or tasks. If they mention Gmail, Google Docs, Google Sheets, CRM, QuickBooks, Excel, ERP, Shopify, eBay, or Facebook Marketplace, mention those exact tools.

If the post asks for a resume:

- Include a "Brief introduction" paragraph.
- Say clearly: "I am not applying as a regular full-time/part-time assistant, so I do not want to misrepresent myself."
- Link to `nathanpuls.com` as background.
- Do not attach a resume unless the user provides one or explicitly asks to attach it.

If Nathan speaks Spanish and the post mentions bilingual:

- Include: "I am also comfortable communicating in Spanish."

## Sending Rules

Use Gmail only when the user explicitly asks to send.

Before sending:

- confirm the destination came from the listing itself
- confirm the message references exact post details
- confirm the message says Nathan is not applying as an employee when that matters
- remove placeholders such as `[phone number]`
- do not attach files unless provided and requested

After sending, report:

- which posts were sent
- which email addresses were used, unless privacy concerns suggest summarizing
- any skipped posts and why
- any changes made to the draft, such as removing a phone placeholder
